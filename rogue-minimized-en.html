Rogue Minimized

A small remake of the core of Rogue (1980), the game that gave its name to a whole genre: a dungeon drawn with characters in the terminal, explored one turn at a time. You build it in C#, one milestone per week, using what you learn in Monday's lesson. Milestones are assigned one at a time.

Final delivery Wed, January 20Individual game, group wiki

The game

You are an adventurer in a dungeon you cannot see. Every step reveals what is around you. Somewhere there is gold, a piece of armor, an enemy, and the way out.

Gameplay loop

  1. You explore: you move one cell per turn, and the dungeon is revealed around you.
  2. You collect what you find: gold raises your score, armor makes you harder to hurt.
  3. You fight the enemy that guards the room with the exit, by walking into it.
  4. You leave the dungeon through the exit. Or you die trying.

Creative freedom

You draw your own dungeon, and you choose characters, colors, the name of the game and of the enemy. What matters is that every mechanic works as described. Rogue Minimized is the name of the project: give your game its own name.

Mechanics

1 · Turn

What it isThe game waits for one key. Every key that does something is one turn: the player acts, then the enemy reacts, then the screen is redrawn.
KeysArrows or WASD to move. M reveals the whole map (debug). Q quits.
RulesA key that does nothing (an unknown key, a move into a wall) does not cost a turn and does not crash the game.

2 · Movement

ConditionsThe destination cell is floor, corridor, door or exit.
EffectsThe player moves by one cell. If the destination has an item, it is collected. If it is the exit, the game is won.
EdgesWalls and empty space cannot be crossed. The player can never leave the map, even at its borders.

3 · Fog of war

StartNo cell is revealed.
Every turnThe player's cell and the 8 cells around it become revealed, and stay revealed forever.
DrawingUnrevealed cells are drawn as spaces. Items and enemies on unrevealed cells are invisible.
DebugM reveals every cell.

4 · Items

Gold bagAdds a random amount of gold, between 10 and 30.
ArmorAdds 2 to the player's armor. Armor reduces the damage the player takes.
CollectingWalking onto the item. It disappears from the map and a message says what you found and how much.

5 · Combat

The enemyOne enemy, standing still, guarding the room with the exit. It has 10 HP and 4 attack.
Player attackTrying to move onto the enemy attacks it instead: random damage between 2 and 5. The player does not move.
Enemy attackAt the end of every turn, if the enemy is alive and next to the player (8 directions), it attacks. Damage = enemy attack − player armor, minimum 1.
Enemy deathAt 0 HP it disappears and drops random loot on its cell: a gold bag or a piece of armor, with equal chance.
Player deathAt 0 HP: game over.
MessagesEvery attack writes a message: who hit whom, and for how much.

6 · HUD and end of game

HUDAlways visible at the bottom of the screen: HP (current / max), Armor, Gold, Turn. The player starts with 20 HP, 0 armor, 0 gold.
Message lineAlways visible at the top: the last thing that happened.
End screenVictory (exit reached) or game over, with gold collected and turns played.

7 · Randomness

Everything random (damage, gold, loot) comes from one Random object created with a seed written at the top of the code. With the same seed and the same keys, the game must play out exactly the same way. That is how it is tested in class.

Nice to have (extra points)

  • Chase: when the player is within 5 cells, the enemy takes one step toward them each turn, along the axis with the greater distance, if that cell is walkable.
  • Room reveal: entering a room lights it up entirely, as in the original Rogue.
  • More enemies and items, using a List.
  • Level from a file: the map is loaded from a .txt file instead of being written in the code.
  • Random dungeon: rooms and corridors generated at the start, the same for the same seed. Every room must be reachable.

Wiki

The game is individual, the documentation is shared: the class writes one wiki together on GitHub and keeps it growing during the module. What goes in it and how it is organized is up to you.

Where to find sources

  • Official C# and .NET documentation on Microsoft Learn: the C# guide for the language, the .NET API reference for classes like Console and Random.
  • About Rogue: the original manual (A Guide to the Dungeons of Doom), RogueBasin (a wiki about roguelikes), and above all your own game sessions.
  • Your own tests: a small program that proves how something behaves is often the best source of all.
  • AI tools: useful to find a starting point or to check a text, but they can be wrong. Verify against one of the sources above.

The wiki has one grade for the whole class.

Technical requirements

  • C# console application, .NET 11, VS Code.
  • Characters: ASCII, plus Unicode box-drawing (┌ ┐ └ ┘ ─ │ ├ ┤ ┬ ┴ ┼), shade blocks (░ ▒ ▓ █) and ·. No emoji or other symbols: they can take two columns and break the grid. Set Console.OutputEncoding to UTF-8 at startup.
  • The screen is redrawn in place, not scrolled: no trace of previous frames.
  • Only what we have covered in class, plus the snippets given with each milestone. Anything else is allowed if you can explain it.
  • A README with: how to run the game, the keys, the seed, the choices you made.
